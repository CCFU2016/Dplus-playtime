How the calculation works

The formulas, ECC's timeline and the assumptions behind the figures
Reserved = base limit today + base limit next business day Excess = Collateral − Reserved − Open risk Dynamic = max(Excess, 0) × split ÷ reset days From 18:00 = base limit today + Dynamic After settlement = From 18:00 + settled purchases × split After a weekend = next auction back at the full limit (lower of two readings)
  1. 16:00: each market's limit resets to its base limit, the fixed amount you set to bridge the time until the evening update. Resting orders above it, including bids for future auctions, are deleted.
  2. Reserved for base limits: ECC holds back today's base limit and the one it will hand out at 16:00 on the next business day, for both markets, because that one goes live before the next calculation can see today's trades.
  3. M7 continuous does not reset on weekends or holidays: the base limit before them is BD + NBD per non-business day, and the evening limit must last until the next business morning. Reset days are always 1.
  4. MATS auctions reset every calendar day. Before a weekend or holiday each auction day gets max(BD, NBD) as base limit and an equal share of the dynamic limit: ÷ 3 on a Friday, because its calculation lasts until Monday, so the Saturday and Sunday auctions each get a third. On the next business day, the day-ahead auction is back at the full limit if those auctions went unused.
  5. Around 18:00 (best effort, no guaranteed time): the dynamic limit is added to what is left of the base limit.
  6. By 10:45 next business day: once yesterday's purchases are paid, that amount comes back, split by your key. If collateral was short, this calculator assumes the payment first refills the reserve; ECC's materials do not say.

Assumptions and limits

  • Formulas follow ECC's DCP Simulation Tool v1.3.5 and ECC's client deck (July 2025). ECC's production system may differ in detail; ECC's calculation always prevails.
  • Limits are shown before any new trades. Trading uses them up; only net buying counts (volume × price), so a sale at a negative price adds to what you owe.
  • Unsettled purchases are assumed to be paid in full the next morning.
  • "Size for the whole week" (the default) finds one split that meets the targets every day. "The weekend only" solves the calculation before the weekend or holiday, as the deck does; the other days are shown with the same split, so their M7 continuous limit can fall below your target, and the tile says what they need with a split of their own.
  • After a weekend or holiday, the next business day's day-ahead auction is back at the full limit, as in ECC's client deck (ECC's simulation tool shows only one day's share there). ECC's worked example fits two readings: the unused weekend or holiday limit carried over, or the normal weekday level. With both markets and weekend base limits they differ by a few thousand euros; the calculator shows the lower one, assuming those auctions went unused.
  • When collateral does not cover the reserved base limits, ECC's tool flags the setup as invalid. Adjust base limits or collateral.
  • ECC business days for 2026–2027 from ECC's calendar. Base limits are used as entered (ECC's tool rounds them up to the next €10).
  • "Optimised by day" sizes each business day's calculation on its own and assumes you change your collateral (and split) every business day, in time for that day's evening calculation. Collateral changes run through ECC's daily payment cycle, so check the timing with ECC or in the Direct+ platform before relying on it.
  • MATS auctions: ECC's clearing manual says the auction limit covers the day-ahead and the intraday auctions. We assume ECC's DCP limit works the same way, one limit for all MATS auctions, until ECC confirms. The auction timings on this page are the day-ahead auction's; intraday auctions draw on the same limit at other times of day.
  • EUR only. GB markets (GBP, FX, delivery risk) are out of scope.
